Pixel-art animation sheet of ONE mech rhino doing a GROUND POUND, on a plain magenta background (#FF00FF). The two attached sprites are the exact design: the first is the three-quarter front view, turned toward the camera and facing down and to the right; the second is the three-quarter back view, turned away from the camera and facing up and to the right. Keep everything from them: big flat hexagonal yellow armour plates with the dark frame showing between them, short stubby legs with big round grey disc wheel joints, flat grey feet, two-tone grey nose horn and a small second horn, red eye, grey jaw, short tail stub, the same colours, outline and chunky pixel size. Only the pose changes. Never redesign: no gloss, no rounded armour, no long or realistic legs, no new parts.

Layout: a grid of 2 rows and 6 columns of equal cells, one whole rhino per cell, nothing crossing a cell edge, no labels, no text, no grid lines. Row 1 is the three-quarter front view facing down and to the right, exactly the camera angle of the first sprite: we see its face and horn. Row 2 is the three-quarter back view facing up and to the right, exactly the camera angle of the second sprite: we see its rump and tail stub, and its head points away. Every cell in a row keeps that row's angle: never turn it to a straight front, side or back view, and never mirror it. Each column is one moment seen from two angles, so the two rows match frame for frame. The rhino is the same size in every cell and the ground line is at the same height in every cell. Its legs keep their length in every view: when the front end rises, the shoulders and head rise above the hips while the hind legs stay planted as they are.

Columns, left to right:
1. Idle: all four feet flat on the ground.
2. Anticipation: weight sinks onto the back legs, the front end starts to rise, the front feet just leave the ground.
3. Rear up: the front legs are lifted well clear of the ground, front feet about a third of the body height above the ground, chest up, standing on the back legs.
4. Peak: the front end at its highest, front legs tucked up and clearly in the air, back feet planted.
5. Slam: the front feet hit the ground hard, body dropped low, legs bent and splayed.
6. Recover: back to the idle stance, all four feet planted.
The back feet stay planted on the ground line in every frame. No motion lines, no dust, no impact effect, no shadow.
