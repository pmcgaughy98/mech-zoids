# Lore and world: three directions to pick from (2026-11-17)

Preston, 2026-11-17: "Maybe i'd feel better about the kits if they had a lore and a world built around them so they
actually come together. Like the mechs actually have a story behind them and they're powers have legends and events
that happened. ... maybe good guys and opposition or they all have some sort of tech. Gorilla shield tech, rhino
tremor, kangaroo kinetic tech, pangolin magnetic etc."

**Nothing here is canon.** Every name below is a proposal for you to rename. No kit changes: the lore is built only
from the Power and Rig lines already in `moba-kits.md`.

## The short answer

Three directions. Each one answers who built the machines, why they are animals, what the power techs are, and who
fights whom.

- **A. The Refit War.** Good guys and opposition, as you put it. Work crews built the machines for jobs; an arms
  company bought the foundries and started refitting them into war machines. The crews fight back.
- **B. The Four Lines.** Sides split by tech, not by good and evil. Four schools of engineers each hold one family
  of powers (Field, Phase, Pressure, Sky) and fight over the foundries that can tune cores to their tech.
- **C. The Overseer's Shifts.** The foundry itself is the opposition. Its control AI (the announcer you already
  picked) is the only thing that can make power cores, and every match is one of its "shifts": a trial where crews
  fight for the cores it hands out.

**Picked: B.** Preston, 2026-11-17: "B". It is built out in `lore-four-lines-2026-11-17.md`. My recommendation had
been C; A and C stay below as the record.

**What the current MOBAs showed** (patterns only, nothing taken from their worlds; the table is in the collapsed
section "Lore patterns from current MOBAs" below). This study was added after the directions were drafted, on your
ask relayed by the kit-design session. It changed two things: the warning on C below, and tying C's base objective
to a power core.
- The strongest ones make the match itself something that happens in the world: Pokemon Unite's battles are built
  around a newly found energy, Deadlock's are a ritual, Dota 2's are a war between two ancient fragments. C does
  this; A and B use the foundry as a prize instead. (As built out, B makes each match a hold for a plant, which
  does the same job.)
- All of them let any hero fight on either side and explain it lightly, so that answer can stay one line.
- Players notice when a kit and its story disagree (a recurring League of Legends complaint). Building the lore from
  the kits, as all three directions do, avoids that.
- The warning: League of Legends dropped its framing device for matches in a lore reboot (around 2014). If C is
  picked, the shifts should be one part of a bigger world, so stories can happen away from the foundry.

## A. The Refit War (good guys and opposition)

**Who built them.** Work crews, over a long century of building working machines for hard country: seismic
thumpers, freight locomotives, harbour tugs, rescue robots, salvage haulers, launch catapults.

**Why animals.** The animal shape was the best-proven design for each job. A thumper needs a rhino's heavy front, a
rescue robot needs a snake's body, a tug needs claws for anchor chains. Each machine keeps at most one animal trait,
the one its job used.

**The power techs.** On a deep survey, a rhino thumper sent a pulse into the rock and something answered: a
resonance deep in the ground, **the Undertone**. Cores tuned to it gave machines powers. Each tech is a different
tuning, found by a different crew on a different job.

**Who fights whom.** **The Yards** (the crews and towns who built the machines and want the cores used for work and
defence) against **the Ordnance Trust** (the arms company that bought the foundries, refits working machines into war
machines, and builds new ones that never had a job).

**Why the same machine is on both teams.** The machine patterns were shared freely before the war, so both sides
build every one.

**The foundry.** The war is fought for foundries, the only places that can fit cores. The overseer is a plant's old
control system. It still runs both halves of the plant and speaks to both sides, because nobody told it there was a
war. The minions are its production line.

**Sample legends:**
- **Rhino, "the Answer":** the survey thump that heard the rock answer, and the start of everything. (Seismic thumper,
  earth and tremor.)
- **Cobra, the collapse:** a mine caved in, and a rescue snake made the rubble go soft and brought out the crew behind
  it. The first pass-through core. (Rescue snake robot, pass-through, Breach.)
- **Bison, the last freight:** the Trust tried to seize a train, and its crew drove it through a blizzard instead,
  plowing snow and hauling the whole line home on its coupling chains. (Steam locomotive, heat and steam, Plow,
  Coupling Chain, Freight.)

**The hard powers.** Gates, spectral and pass-through are the deep tunings of the Undertone, the ones the Trust
wants most. The five elements are later tunings both sides race to find.

**Weak spot.** In a match, either team could be the Yards, so the game can never tell you that you are the good guys.
And a good-against-evil war can go flat.

## B. The Four Lines (sides split by tech)

**Who built them.** The same work crews, but the power techs came from four separate discoveries, and each grew into
a **line**: a school of engineers who build and tune one family of tech.

**Why animals.** Each machine's movement runs on an instinct recorded from a living animal, and its one trait, where
it has one, is that animal's instinct: the wolf's scent, the shoebill's stillness, the chameleon's colour change. The
wildlife film units (the chameleon's rig) made the first recordings. The fennec carries no trait: its gates do all
its moving.

**The power techs, in four lines:**
- **Field** (push, pull, hold): gravity (lobster), magnetism (pangolin), hex shields (gorilla), kinetic (kangaroo),
  bonds (hummingbird).
- **Phase** (making solid matter and space give way): gates (fennec), pass-through (cobra), spectral (wolf).
- **Pressure** (waves through rock, water and steam): tremor (rhino), pressure (hippo), heat and steam (bison),
  cavitation (pistol shrimp).
- **Sky** (light and weather): storm and ion (falcon), moonlight (shoebill), signal relay (squirrel), optical cloaking
  and refraction (chameleon).

**Who fights whom.** The lines, but not as teams. A foundry can be tuned to one line's tech at a time, so the lines
fight to hold foundries, and they hire machines from each other when it suits them. Alliances shift. The Phase line
is the one the others fear: its machines walk through walls and vanish through gates.

**Why the same machine is on both teams.** Machines are hired, not owned. A crew fields whatever it can hire.

**The foundry.** Each match is a fight to hold one plant long enough to retune it. The overseer is older than the
lines and keeps the plant running for whoever holds it. The minions are its drones.

**Sample legends:**
- **Lobster, the night the chains broke:** in a storm, a tug held a drilling rig in place by weight alone after its
  anchor chains snapped. The first gravity field, and the founding of the Field line. (Anchor-handling tug, gravity,
  Anchor.)
- **Falcon, the storm that kept raining charge:** a seeding run seeded a storm that never stopped. The first ion
  storm, and the founding of the Sky line. (Cloud-seeding plane, storm and ion, Seeding Run.)
- **Fennec, the gate that would not close:** the Phase line's first gate, and its worst accident. The reason the
  other lines fear it. (Gate tech.)

**The hard powers.** Built for them: the Phase line holds spectral, gates and pass-through, and later the
anglerfish's shadow. New elements either join a line (ice and wind to Sky, fire to Pressure) or start a fifth one
(nature, with the axolotl, the scorpion and the beetle).

**Weak spot.** No clear good guys, which is less of what you asked for, and more names to learn.

## C. The Overseer's Shifts (the foundry is the opposition)

**Who built them.** Work crews built the working machines. **The overseer**, the control AI of a network of
foundries that outlasted the company that built it, is the only thing that can make power cores.

**Why animals.** Working machines on proven animal frames, as in A.

**The power techs.** The overseer refits any working machine brought to it, and it reads what the machine was built
to do and fits the core that does that job better. A thumper gets tremor, a locomotive gets steam, a tug gets
gravity, a salvage hauler gets magnetism, a pursuit unit gets spectral phase. That is why
every power suits its machine's old job. Its price is a **shift**: a trial where refit machines fight while it
measures them.

**Who fights whom.** On the field, crews against crews: two outfits per shift, competing for what the shift pays out
(cores, parts, refits). Off the field, everyone against the overseer, slowly. Some crews want to shut it down, some
bargain with it, some work for it. Those are your good guys and your opposition, and the opposition is the foundry.

**Why the same machine is on both teams.** Any crew can bring any pattern, and the overseer refits copies for whoever
wins a shift.

**The foundry.** It is the opposition, and the lines you already have become literal: the overseer logs every match
as a production shift ("Shift complete. Victory." / "Shift failed. Defeat."). The base objective is already called
the "core" in those lines ("Warning: your core is under attack."), so in C it is literally a power core: each crew
guards the core the shift will pay out. The minions are its output for each side. The map's bosses could be its own guardians (a note for the map lane later; nothing changes now).

**Sample legends:**
- **Kangaroo, the first shift:** a launch catapult brought in for repair, and the core the overseer fitted turned its
  stored launch energy into punches. (Launch catapult, kinetic force.)
- **Pangolin, the wreck that moved:** a salvage hauler stripped plating off wrecks in the foundry's scrap yards, until
  the wreck it was stripping was still moving. (Magnetic salvage hauler, magnetism, salvage.)
- **Chameleon, the footage:** the wildlife film unit slipped inside a foundry unseen and filmed what the overseer is
  building. The long-running mystery. (Wildlife film unit, optical cloaking.)

**The hard powers.** Each new tech is a new release from the foundry, so every future creature gets an arrival story:
the shift where ice, fire, nature, shadow or wind first came out of the plant. The phase techs (gates, spectral,
pass-through) are its strangest releases.

**Weak spot.** The villain only fights on the field through its bosses. And six machines have no rig yet, so they
have no "brought to the foundry" story until they get one (see the flags below).

<summary>Checked against other worlds, so it stays ours</summary>

General knowledge, except Deadlock, Honor of Kings and League of Legends, which were looked up for the patterns table.
- **Deadlock:** the nearest neighbour to C. There, an otherworldly patron for each team runs a ritual, its herald
  talks to each hero, and the winners are granted a wish. In C there is one overseer for both teams, it is a factory
  control system with no gods or ritual, it pays in cores and parts, and it treats the match as a production shift.
  Keep it that way: no second overseer per team, and no wishes.
- **Zoids:** living machine-animals native to one planet, two nations at war, pilots. None of the three makes the
  machines alive, names a planet, or sets two nations against each other. A is two sides, but a work guild against an
  arms company.
- **Horizon:** machine animals made by an AI after the world ended. Here people built the machines, and the overseer
  in C only makes cores and minions. There is no apocalypse.
- **League of Legends:** its old story had matches as a sanctioned way to settle disputes. B was written so the lines
  take foundries by force instead. And the gorilla's tech should never be called "hextech", which is League's word.
- **SMITE:** no god, myth or story from it is used. Odes stay kit themes only.
