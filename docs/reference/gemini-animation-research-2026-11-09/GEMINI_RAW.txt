Biomechanical and Technical Animation Research Report: Quadruped and Bipedal Animal-Mech MOBA Framework
Execution Research Plan
The objective of this research is to establish an implementation-ready, highly reusable animation authoring and verification framework for a Godot 4.7.2 animal-mech MOBA1. This framework eliminates reliance on subjective director direction for basic animal actions while establishing precise technical criteria for integrating generative motion tools, specifically Meshy Text to Motion1. The execution plan consists of eight sequential phases designed to resolve motion plausibility, structural armor constraints, and real-time responsiveness1.

	Biomechanical literature confirms that massive quadrupeds divide locomotor labor unevenly between forelimbs and hindlimbs9. Forelimbs absorb up to 65% of total static body weight and execute the primary braking impulses during deceleration, while hindlimbs deliver forward propulsion9. Furthermore, gait selection correlates directly with duty factor (  ): quadrupeds maintain    during walking gaits, whereas running gaits introduce duty factors below 0.5, creating brief aerial phases or compliant stance flexures8.
When translating these biological facts to game mechs, animators must recognize that AI motion generators (such as Meshy) are trained primarily on bipedal human motion capture data2. Consequently, raw generative outputs cannot natively express quadruped force distribution, ground reaction forces (GRF), or rigid-body mechanical clearances without structured retargeting and manual IK reconstruction2.

The kinematic sequence follows Left Hind (LH) contact    Left Fore (LF) contact    Right Hind (RH) contact    Right Fore (RF) contact8. Mechanically, the spine remains rigid along the roll axis, expressing weight transfer through heavy vertical suspension travel in the shoulder girdles4. Forelimbs bear 65% of dynamic load9.

Diagonal limb pairs move in near-synchrony: (LH + RF) strike together, followed by a brief suspension phase (  ), then (RH + LF) strike together8. Vertical torso displacement increases; suspension springs bottom out during diagonal contact phases, creating high mechanical energy returns15.

Meshy Text to Motion Trial Protocol and API Constraints
Official API Specifications and Technical Limits
The Meshy Text to Motion API operates under strict technical constraints that govern generation formats, execution costs, and output characteristics2.
The API accepts HTTP POST requests at /openapi/v1/text-to-motion2. Text prompts are strictly capped at 400 characters, with longer inputs returning HTTP 400 errors2. Generated clip durations range from 2.0 to 10.0 seconds, enforced on 0.5-second step increments2. The API provides two operational modes: Prime mode costs 10 credits per request and outputs motion baked onto an FBX skeleton, while Swift mode costs 3 credits per request and returns raw BVH motion data2. The output skeleton defaults to a standard 24-joint humanoid structure (SMPL-H naming convention) scaled to human heights of 1.7m to 1.8m10.
Mismatch Resolution: Sub-Second Cast Windows vs. Minimum Clip Duration
MOBA gameplay requires ability activation times (cast windows) between 0.20s and 0.45s to ensure competitive responsiveness3. However, the Meshy API enforces a minimum clip generation duration of 2.0 seconds2.
To resolve this mismatch, generated 2.0s clips must undergo phase isolation:
                     1. Extract the core 0.3s action phase (e.g., peak strike or shield thrust)3.
                     2. Discard the pre-roll anticipation and post-roll recovery tails generated by the AI3.
                     3. Retarget the isolated 0.3s action slice onto the project's native rig3.
                     4. Time-scale keyframes in Blender to align exact impact frames with gameplay logic events3.
Native Rig Preservation and Retargeting Pipeline
Generative motion endpoints output motion baked onto standard 24-joint humanoid skeletons10. Replacing the project's approved gorilla/rhino meshes with auto-rigged AI outputs is strictly prohibited1. The native mesh, weight paint allocations, rigid armor groupings, and rest poses must remain untouched1. Meshy outputs serve exclusively as animation donor files, transferred via DCC retargeting onto our original rig3.

Blender 5.2 Control Rig Setup and Constraint Architecture
The Blender retargeting workflow imports donor FBX/BVH files, aligns rest pose axes, maps bone hierarchies, applies hand and foot IK locks, solves constrained motion, bakes FK to master rig controls, and fixes armor collisions prior to exporting GLB assets to Godot1.
                     1. Rest Axis Alignment: Match donor rest transforms to native mech rest poses prior to baking3. Mismatched roll axes produce spatial twisting in shoulder and knee joints3.
                     2. IK/FK Contact Locking: Apply Blender IK Limbs constraints to forelimbs during stance phases3. Set IK influence to 1.0 during ground contact and 0.0 during swing phases3.
                     3. Rigid Armor Constraints: Apply Limit Rotation constraints to armor bone channels to prevent plates from rotating past mechanical stops:
                     * Shoulder Pauldrons: Max pitch   , Max roll   1.
                     * Forearm Shields: Max yaw    relative to lower arm bone1.
Godot 4.7 AnimationTree and Runtime Playback Architecture
In Godot 4.7, animation state execution relies on an AnimationNodeBlendTree containing integrated sub-graphs3. Locomotion blend spaces connect through an AnimationNodeOneShot node to trigger ability and attack clips before sending output to the engine root3.
Blending Strategy Tradeoffs: Contact-Aware Blending vs. Inertialization
Standard linear cross-fading (AnimationNodeBlend2) interpolates bone positions linearly over time, causing planted stance feet to slide across the ground during transitions3. To maintain rigid ground contact, Godot 4.7 setups must choose between two strategies based on transition speed3:
                        1. Phase-Synchronized Blending: Used for continuous locomotion transitions (e.g., walk to trot). Normalizes stride phase (  ) so foot-strike timings align prior to blending8.
                        2. Inertialization: Used for abrupt skill interrupts (e.g., walk to Shield Drive). Upon state transition, source pose and bone velocities are captured; decaying spring functions blend these offsets into the target animation over 0.08–0.12 seconds3. This eliminates cross-fade float while maintaining kinetic forward momentum3.
Frame-Rate Independent Event Execution
Gameplay code must trigger visual effects, audio clips, and damage frames reliably regardless of dynamic frame-rate drops or time-scale adjustments (e.g., slow-motion buffs)3.
Never use polling timers (await get_tree().create_timer()) in GDScript to sync combat hits. Utilize Godot AnimationPlayer Call Method Tracks placed directly on exact contact keyframes3. Set AnimationTree.process_callback = ANIMATION_PROCESS_PHYSICS to ensure frame evaluation updates in sync with fixed physics ticks3.

Generative Limitations
Automated text-to-motion tools cannot be trusted to independently solve four core technical requirements of this project1:
                              1. Rigid Armor Mesh Clearances: AI generators operate on deforming skin meshes and do not compute physical hard-surface interpenetration between rigid metal armor plates1.
                              2. Explicit World-Locked Stance Contacts: Generative clips exhibit stance drift and foot sliding, requiring manual IK locking and root motion extraction in Blender3.
                              3. Two-Body Interaction Constraints: Complex multi-character actions (such as the Grab/Lift/Ground Slam) require precise constraint attachment points between offender and victim rigs that text prompts cannot specify1.
                              4. Sub-Second Gameplay Cast Timing: Generative models cannot output sub-second (<0.4s) clips natively due to API minimum clip limits (2.0s), requiring manual phase extraction and keyframe scaling2.
Compact Operating Checklist
                              * Step 1 (Rest Transforms): Verify rest transform alignment between donor skeleton and native mech rig in Blender 5.23.
                              * Step 2 (Geometry Preservation): Ensure zero mesh stretching, bone scaling, or rigid armor plate deformation during posing1.
                              * Step 3 (IK Stance Clamp): Apply IK constraints to clamp world-space stance foot/knuckle slip to under 0.005 m1.
                              * Step 4 (Armor Clearances): Audit rigid armor plate clearances at 0.25x speed, maintaining 15 mm minimum separation1.
                              * Step 5 (Closed Fist Contacts): Ensure chest beat and swat contacts utilize flat palm-side mechanical fist surfaces1.
                              * Step 6 (Exit Timing Discipline): Enforce accepted exit timing—complete final strikes before initiating gathering arcs1.
                              * Step 7 (Cast Window Isolation): Trim 2.0s minimum generative clips to sub-second gameplay cast windows2.
                              * Step 8 (Multi-Camera Audit): Inspect assets at 1.0x and 0.25x across Front, Side, and 45-degree MOBA camera views1.
                              * Step 9 (Physics Signal Binding): Attach VFX, audio, and damage triggers directly to Godot AnimationPlayer Call Method Tracks3.
