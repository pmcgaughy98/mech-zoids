# First Meshy donor comparison

Executed once after Preston's "do it" authorization. See **MESHY_TRIAL_RESULT.md** for the 10-credit result, visual comparison and rejection decision. No automatic retries ran.

## One first experiment

One **Prime, 2.0-second** protective forearm gesture. Actual cost: **10 Meshy credits**. No automatic retries or batches. This is a donor-motion test, not a claim that Meshy can generate our exact gorilla rig, quadruped support or shield geometry. Balance was 1,020 before and 1,010 after.

Proposed prompt, under 400 characters:

> A heavy armored character stands with feet planted in a wide low stance, draws the right forearm across the chest, then pushes it outward once in a protective shield gesture and returns to the starting pose. Clear loaded preparation, decisive outward release, controlled recovery. No walking, jumping, spinning or repeated attacks.

Use the resulting upper-body movement as a candidate only. Inspect the raw donor's skeleton, units, frame rate, useful action window and root movement. Compare its action read before spending time on adaptation.

## Local adaptation

Import the standalone FBX into Blender without modifying any approved source. Save a separate working copy. Match rest axes and bone roles from the actual file. Retarget the useful motion onto a copy of the current gorilla, preserving original bone lengths, geometry, weights and textures. Keep the authored support stance where a human donor cannot supply a gorilla's knuckle support. Add explicit contact/clearance corrections where needed and log the time spent.

The source shield-cast baseline is 0.6 seconds with release at 0.25 seconds. Do not compress the entire two-second generated clip blindly into that duration. Identify and adapt its useful preparation/thrust/recovery phases, then compare the resulting timing and readability against the authored baseline. Preserve the existing release contract and recovery cancellation behavior in a runtime trial.

## Evidence and decision

Deliver raw donor playback, fitted body-only playback, and a same-camera comparison with the existing cast, each at normal and labelled slower speed. Check support drift, hand/shield/chest clearance, rigid lengths, intended action and smooth recovery. Record generation cost, adaptation time and remaining faults.

Useful means clearer or cheaper to finish than our current authored motion. A playable export, generic human gesture, smooth curve or attractive effect alone does not establish an upgrade. One Prime sample cannot establish Prime/Swift superiority; any later matched comparison must use the same action/settings where possible and its own authorized cost cap.
