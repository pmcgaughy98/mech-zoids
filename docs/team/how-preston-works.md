# How Preston works (carried over from Hollowtide, 2026-10-11)

Hollowtide's sessions learned these from Preston's corrections over many weeks. A new session starts without
them; read this once and apply it. Where this project has decided otherwise, `CLAUDE.md` and `START_HERE.md`
win.

## Showing work
- **Show, don't describe.** After building something visual, record it (`--write-movie`, then ffmpeg to mp4)
  and send him the video, or a still for a small change. He judges by watching.
- **Look before you claim.** Grab frames from the recording and look at them yourself first. A check that
  passes can still test nothing; Hollowtide had characters "visible" in a check while off screen in the game.
- **Keep him in the loop on long work:** a short line on what you're doing, then the result.
- When he names a position ("next to the firefly", "down a little and in the middle"), measure it in pixels
  against the art and confirm with a frame. Several small rounds is normal; each one gets a frame.

## Writing (dialogue, cards, item text)
- Draft real lines, labelled as proposals; he edits. Bracketed placeholder text reads as unfinished and
  hides problems.
- His voice target: people from St. Joseph, Missouri. Plain, warm, a little wry. Characters **react before
  they inform** and never just announce the next step ("he said it sounded like caveman talk" when lines
  only described the next action). Example of the register: "Something just told me I needed to come down
  here tonight."
- Study finished games' scripts for rhythm (synthesis), never copy their lines.

## Art
- **No "AI look."** Pixel art stays on its grid at whole-number scale; nothing smooth, smeared or invented.
  Generated art (Retro Diffusion, PixelLab, Higgsfield) is checked frame by frame and forced back onto the
  grid and palette when needed.
- Palettes are designed, not natural: Hollowtide moved every creature off Earth-animal colours. Here he wants
  machines close to Zoids in animals and faction palette, within the line in `CLAUDE.md`.
- Characters keep their job gear standing; walking versions drop held objects and put the arms down; wild or
  battle versions drop gear. Back views must match the front (tail placement, markings).
- One hand-made look across UI: pixel 9-slice frames, fonts by job, no flat default boxes in finished UI.
- Codex finishes art into slots; Claude wires the slots. Codex works from a written task list with sizes.

## Synthesis and research
- "Use synthesis" = study finished, human-made games first (method in `CLAUDE.md`). He also links
  disassemblies and videos "for synthesis": structure only, never code, data, text, art or audio.
- When he says something feels off, go back to the references rather than guessing.

## Money and tools
- Paid generators: say the cost first, run one job at a time, never re-run a paid job automatically.
  Hollowtide rates (2026-10): Retro Diffusion edit $0.18, rotation $0.10, walk $0.14 per facing; ElevenLabs
  about 17 credits per sound effect, 900 per song; Higgsfield Kling 3.0 5-second clip 7.5 credits.
- Music and sound are tuned to 432 Hz in Hollowtide; ask before assuming the same here.
- He approves downloads. Never enter his passwords or keys anywhere.

## Playtest lessons from Hollowtide's pilot
- The player must always know where to go: an objective line on screen from minute one.
- Every story area needs a way back in after the story moves on.
- Keep Play (the main scene) on the title screen.
- Test chains must go through the real scene changes a player would.
- Short floors and early dungeons felt too short; he asked for more floors before the first exit.

## Working style
- He is the director: names, lore, designs and numbers are his. Offer proposals with the alternative named,
  then build what he picks.
- One working thing per session, verified. No urgency, no hype, no em-dashes.
- Commit only when asked, as "Preston McGaughy Jr <pmcgaughy98@gmail.com>", ending with the Co-Authored-By line.
